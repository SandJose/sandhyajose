Sandhya Jose

Project Scientist-II, Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology, Pune

“I study how tiny particles in the air decide when fog forms, how dense it gets, and when it lifts.”

Hi, I am a Project Scientist-II at the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM), Pune, where I work with the Winter Fog Experiment (WIFEX) group on fog microphysics, aerosol–fog interactions, and better fog forecasts. Before this, I was a Project Scientist at the India Meteorological Department (IMD), Pune, working on operational forecasting and climate monitoring. I earned my Ph.D. in Atmospheric Sciences at CSIR–National Physical Laboratory (NPL) under Dr. Sachchidanand Singh, and have also done research at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), IITM and the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL).

My research focuses on understanding aerosol–cloud–radiation interactions and aerosol influences on fog formation and evolution. My doctoral research investigated aerosol–cloud–radiation interactions over Indo-Gangetic Plains, particularly in monsoonal and pre-monsoonal cloud systems, with an emphasis on aerosol particle size distributions and their influence on cloud properties and radiative effects. Building on this foundation, my current work examines the microphysical evolution of fog, particularly the transition from haze to fog over the Indo-Gangetic Plain, and the role of aerosol properties in modulating fog formation, development, and dissipation. I am also interested in evaluating and developing techniques to improve numerical weather prediction models and enhance operational weather prediction and forecasting capabilities. My broader research interests include new particle formation (NPF), aerosol chemical transformation, secondary inorganic aerosol formation, aerosol water uptake and hydration, and cloud condensation nuclei (CCN) activation. Through my research, I aim to advance the understanding of aerosol–cloud–fog interactions and their implications for weather prediction and climate, while contributing to climate change research, mitigation, and scientific awareness.
